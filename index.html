Report an absenceReportar una ausencia
In short: Absences are excused with a phone call or written note from the parent/guardian. If it isn't received within 2 days of the absence, the absence stays unexcused. For a planned extended absence, inform the office at least a week in advance with a written note. (Student handbooks)

Elementary, middle and high school (all three 2026–27 student handbooks)

  • Absences must be excused with a phone call or written note from the parent/guardian. If the call or note is not received within 2 days of the absence, it stays unexcused.
  • Parents/guardians may excuse a student up to five times per semester without documentation. Absences beyond those five need a doctor's note to be excused.
  • An extended absence planned ahead: inform the office at least a week in advance with a written note from the parent/guardian, for the principal's approval. The student is responsible for completing all work before the absence.
  • You are notified of absences through the Infinite Campus parent portal, but only if you have signed up for it.
  • Elementary and middle school: the school will notify you in writing after 3 unexcused absences in a row, 5 in a semester or 7 in a year.

High school also says (2026–27 WHS Handbook)

  • A parent/guardian must call the school if the student is going to be absent.
  • A student is counted absent for a class period if they miss 15 or more minutes of it for any reason.
  • A student may bring a note the following morning to get an absence excused.
  • WHS will notify parents in writing at 3 consecutive or 5 total unexcused absences in a semester, and will refer students to the district attorney or DCF at 7 unexcused absences in a year.

Board policy (JBD)

  • Excused absences include personal illness; health treatment; serious illness or death in the family; obligatory religious observances; district-approved activities; and absences prearranged by parents and approved by the principal. All others are unexcused.
  • Missing two or more hours of a school day counts as an absence for a significant part of the day.
  • It is the student's responsibility to get make-up work from teachers.

Pick up my child earlyRecoger a mi hijo temprano
In short: The principal will only release a student during the school day with a written or verbal request from the student's parent or person acting as a parent (JBH). Elementary and middle school: the parent signs the student out in the office. High school: the student signs out in the office. (Handbooks)

All schools (board policy JBH): The principal will only release a student during the school day with a written or verbal request from the student's lawful parent or person acting as a parent.

Elementary (2026–27 Elementary Handbook)

  • Parents must check in at the office and sign the register before students are called to the office for dismissal.
  • Students may be checked out to a parent/guardian at the start of lunch, but must be checked back in before lunch ends. If not, they are marked tardy; if they don't return, they are counted absent.
  • Dismissal is 3:25 PM. Children not picked up by 3:35 go to the after-school program, and the family pays that program's fee. After the third late pick-up, the school will contact the appropriate authorities.

Middle school (2026–27 WMS Handbook)

  • A parent/guardian needs to sign the student out in the office if leaving during the school day.
  • Students are not allowed to leave the building until they have checked out through the office.

High school (2026–27 WHS Handbook)

  • A student must sign out in the office any time they leave the building during the school day.
  • A student must sign in at the office any time they enter the building after the school day has begun.

See or correct my child's recordsVer o corregir el expediente de mi hijo
In short: Parents/guardians may inspect their child's records during regular school office hours. Parents/guardians may have a hearing to challenge records that are inaccurate, misleading or violate the student's privacy or other rights. (JR, JRD)

Board policy (JR, JRD)

  • Parents/guardians may inspect their child's records during regular school office hours.
  • Parents/guardians may have a hearing to challenge the content of the records, to make sure they are not inaccurate, misleading or a violation of the student's privacy or other rights, and to have them corrected or deleted. You may also insert your own written explanation into the record.
  • The hearing is run by a person with no direct interest in the outcome. You get advance notice of the date, time and place, may bring anyone to help you (including an attorney, at your own expense), and get a written decision.

Ask for a special education or 504 evaluationPedir una evaluación de educación especial o 504
In short: The district provides a free appropriate public education to every exceptional child (IDACA) and makes reasonable modifications under Section 504 (IDACB). Questions about how a student may receive additional support can go to the building principal or the Director of Special Education. (Elementary Handbook)

Board policy (IDACA, IDACB, JQL)

  • The district provides a free appropriate public education to every exceptional child, in the least restrictive environment.
  • The district maintains a system for students who are referred to determine eligibility for special education, following federal and state law.
  • Parents are referred to the Kansas State Department of Education's "Procedural Safeguards in Parent Rights in Special Education."
  • Under Section 504, the district makes reasonable modifications so students with disabilities can take part in its programs. No one may retaliate against a person for using their Section 504 rights.
  • A hearing procedure is available to parents of exceptional students.

Elementary (2026–27 Elementary Handbook)

  • Questions about how a student may receive additional support can be directed to the building principal or to the Director of Special Education.

The policies and handbooks do not spell out how to request an evaluation.

If my child is suspendedSi suspenden a mi hijo
In short: Short-term suspension: except in an emergency, the student first gets notice of the charges and an informal hearing; if not held first, it must be held within 72 hours. The parent/guardian gets written notice within 24 hours. A suspension over 10 days or an expulsion can be appealed in writing within 10 calendar days of receiving the hearing results. (JDD)

Board policy (JDD)

  • Short-term (up to 10 school days): except in an emergency, the student must first get oral or written notice of the charges and an informal hearing. If the hearing isn't held first, it must be held within 72 hours. The parent/guardian gets written notice within 24 hours after the suspension.
  • At the informal hearing the student has the right to be present, hear the charges and the basis for them, and make statements in their defense.
  • Long-term (up to 90 school days) or expulsion: a hearing by a board-authorized hearing officer comes first. You get written notice of the time, date and place, and written notice of the result within 24 hours after it is decided.
  • Appeal: for a suspension over 10 days or an expulsion, file written notice of appeal with the board clerk within 10 calendar days of receiving the hearing results. The appeal is heard within 20 calendar days, with at least 5 calendar days' written notice, and the board decides no later than its next regular meeting.
  • A student will be told the day they can return to school.

Raise a concern or file a complaintPresentar una queja
In short: The building principal tries to resolve complaints informally. If it isn't resolved, you may file a formal written complaint within 10 school days after the informal process ends. Either side may appeal within 20 days after the written decision. (KN)

Board policy (KN)

  • The board encourages complaints to be resolved at the lowest possible administrative level. The building principal tries to resolve them informally and documents the result.
  • If it isn't resolved, you may file a formal written complaint within 10 school days after the informal process ends. Include your name and address and briefly describe the problem. Forms are in each school office and the central office.
  • The investigator issues a written decision. If it won't be done within 45 days of filing, you get written notice with an expected deadline.
  • Either side may appeal within 20 days after the written decision.

Ask for a review of a book or class materialPedir la revisión de un libro o material
In short: Meet with the teacher, media specialist (librarian) or principal about your concern. If it can't be resolved, the principal asks you to complete a Request for Review form. (IF)

Board policy (IF)

  • Meet with the teacher, media specialist (librarian) or principal about your concern.
  • If it can't be resolved, the principal notifies the superintendent and asks you to complete a Request for Review form, available from principals or the district office.
  • The superintendent reviews the request. If it isn't resolved there, you may ask the board to review it. The board decides whether to send it to a review committee.
  • A review committee reports to the board within 30 days of getting the material (the board may extend this; reviews pause during summer and breaks of a week or more). The board acts within 30 days of the report, and its decision is final.

In short: Requests must be made in writing to the official custodian of district records (the board clerk). The request must be acted on no later than the end of the third business day after it is received. The district may charge a fee. (CN)

Board policy (CN)

  • Requests must be made in writing to an official custodian of district records. The board clerk is the custodian.
  • The request must be acted on as soon as possible, and no later than the end of the third business day after it is received. If not acted on right away, the custodian tells you when and where the record will be available.
  • If the request is denied, you can ask for a written explanation, due within three days. If you disagree, the superintendent (the district's Freedom of Information Officer) settles the dispute.
  • The district may charge a fee, and require it in advance, for access or copies.

In short: At open forum, the board president may ask attendees if they would like to speak and may set a time limit (BCBI). To speak as an agenda item, return the Request to Add an Agenda Item form at least 5 days before the meeting; presentations may not exceed 3 minutes. (District form and website)

Board policy (BCBI)

Weather and school closingsClima y cierres de escuelas

Board policy EBBD: When the superintendent believes the safety of students is threatened by severe weather, health or safety concerns, or other circumstances, parents and students will be notified of school closings or cancellations by announcements over radio/TV, text alerts and social media. If an emergency interferes with the school day, students may be dismissed only by the superintendent or the superintendent's designee.

All three 2026–27 student handbooks: In the event of inclement weather, school may be closed or starting time delayed. Check KAKE, KWCH, KSN, the school website (usd353.com), School Status, or social media for school closings or delays.

Middle school handbook: During inclement weather, students wait in the designated area for their grade level until dismissed by administration.

2026–27 district calendar: Any make-up days for weather closings will be scheduled the week of May 24–28, 2027.
